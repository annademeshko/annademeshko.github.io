A feature concept for BeReal Memories

Same Day.

Different places.
One day you both lived.

Pick a day from your Memories when a friend posted too. If they share their side, both photos land in your chat. If you both like it, post it together.

Use the app as
Why Same DayProblem, bets, first test

A small reason
to text a friend again.

Memories is a private archive, and most people scroll it alone. But a lot of those days were days your friends posted too. Same Day uses that overlap as an excuse to reach out to a friend you’ve drifted from, with very little effort and nothing awkward about it.

Key design decisions

01

The reveal happens in the chat, not on its own screen.

A full-screen reveal would look nicer, but it ends in a dead end. In the chat, the reply field is right under the two photos, and a reply is the whole point.

02

Blur, not an empty lock.

Jo sees that Maya’s photo is already waiting for her, the same way BeReal’s “Post to view” works. An empty placeholder gives no reason to tap.

03

Send a date, not a photo.

The invite shows nothing until Jo agrees. Saying no costs nothing and reveals nothing, so the invite doesn’t feel like pressure.

04

Posting together needs a yes from both.

A joint post publishes someone else’s photo, so the other friend sees the exact post and audience before it goes live, and either one can take it down.

The job

Who
Close friends who moved to different cities, still post on BeReal, and talk less than they’d like.
What they want
To restart a conversation with a friend who lives far away, without it feeling forced, so the friendship doesn’t fade.
When it comes up
Scrolling Memories, or thinking of someone and realising it’s been months.
What they do now
Send an old photo or a “miss you” text. Often they send nothing.
Why it falls flat
One photo is one-sided. You get “haha cute” back and the chat goes quiet. Texting out of nowhere feels awkward.
How we’d know it works
It takes seconds to start, doesn’t feel needy, the friend replies on their own, and the chat keeps going after the first reply.
What to take away
The awkwardness of writing first, and the worry of being left on read.
01

Asking is easier than writing.

An invite has nothing to compose and shows nothing until the friend says yes. Both people share, so each gets something new to react to, which is more to talk about than a single photo.

02

The moment to get right: the reveal.

Your morning coffee next to their walk home, from the same date. The friend can see their reward is waiting (blurred, like BeReal’s “Post to view”), and the reveal happens in the chat, right where the reply goes.

03

For BeReal: more from what’s already there.

It runs on two things people already have, Memories and private chat, and asks for no new content. If it works, old photos become conversations between friends who drifted apart. That still has to be proven.

What could kill it, and how to check first

  1. Friends who live apart rarely post on the same day.

    Then the feature almost never shows up.

    Check

    Query existing friend pairs in different cities: how many shared posting days they have, and how recent the last one is. Nothing to build.

  2. Two photos don’t get more replies than one.

    This is the whole idea. If it’s wrong, nothing else matters.

    Check

    A manual test with 8–12 pairs, described below. We pair the photos by hand, so there’s nothing to build.

  3. Invites feel like pressure.

    A request that’s hard to turn down can hurt the friendship it’s meant to help.

    Check

    In the same sessions, ask how the invite felt and whether they’d say no. Count declines and mutes separately.

How I’d run it

Use real shared dates and let people decide whether to send anything. Swap the order of the two formats between pairs and don’t nudge anyone to reply. Watch whether a real reply comes within 48 hours of the reveal and whether the chat keeps going. Log invites, accepts, declines and replies separately, then ask what made it worth answering, or not.

Agree on the bar before starting: if two photos don’t beat one for most pairs, stop here instead of polishing the flow. It’s a small exploratory test to decide the next step, not proof of a retention lift.

Posting together

After both sides are revealed, either friend can suggest posting the pair. It shows up as one post from both of them. Audience works like a normal BeReal (only our friends, our friends and their friends, or everyone), plus one extra option for a shared post: mutual friends only. The other friend sees exactly how the post will look and who will see it, and has to approve. Either of them can take it down for both. Removing the Same Day removes the post too.

The prototype ends in the feed. Switch between Lea, Sam, Kai and Noor to see who gets the post and where: in My Friends, in Friends of Friends, on a public profile, or not at all. This is a second idea with its own numbers (how many pairs post, how often it’s declined). Replies within 48 hours stay the main signal.

Privacy and edge cases

Only days where both friends could see each other’s post at the time count, so “posted that day” doesn’t reveal anything new. The invite shows the date, never the photo. The blurred preview should be a tiny degraded thumbnail made on the server. Location stays off by default.

Invites expire after 7 days. A declined day can’t be sent again by the same person. If either friend deletes the original post or blocks the other, the pair disappears. Removing it can’t undo screenshots.

Ideas I dropped
  • A suggested reply in the feed. Easy to try, but close to replies that already exist in chat.
  • A weekly group video recap. Needs several people to post and agree.
  • A 24-hour story. BeReal already has its own rule for how long posts stay up, so the joint post follows it.
Design notes

Built for iPhone 17 Pro (402 × 874 pt) following Apple’s HIG: SF type, 44 pt touch targets, sheets for side tasks, confirmations only for destructive actions. Components, colours and copy patterns are matched to the current BeReal iOS app, from a set of June 2026 screens.
